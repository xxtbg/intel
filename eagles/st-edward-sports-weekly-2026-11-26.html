1. Top story: Eagles rout Mentor 45–3 — sixth straight regional title

Final — OHSAA Division I, Region 1 final (Fri, Nov 20, Byers Field, Parma): St. Edward 45, Mentor 3
Quarter by quarter (per the team’s postgame graphic): St. Edward 14–10–14–7; Mentor 3–0–0–0. Eagles improve to 12–1; Mentor finishes 10–3.
  • A touchdown on the very first play: Caleb Hall returned the opening kickoff for a touchdown — Kee On Sports called it a 100-yard return that tied a school record; the Plain Dealer / Spectrum coverage put it at 99 yards. The distance differs by source, the impact did not: 7–0 before the offense took a snap. Three plays later Langston Lucas hit Joe Moore on a 59-yard touchdown pass, and it was 14–0 with 8:51 still left in the first quarter (Kee On Sports).
  • All three phases scored: A pick-six by Rowan Kelley (54 yards, Kee On Sports), a Dallas Nixon field goal for a 24–3 halftime lead, rushing touchdowns, a passing touchdown, and Hall’s kickoff return — a complete team win, exactly as head coach Tom Lombardo described it afterward.
  • Lucas leads the offense (Kee On Sports line): Langston Lucas ran 10 times for 105 yards and a touchdown — his 9-yard third-quarter score pushed the lead to 38–3 and started the running clock — and went 7-of-11 passing for 92 yards and a touchdown. Ronnie Neal added 66 yards and a touchdown on 9 carries, and Javyon Woods closed it out with 42 yards and a touchdown on 6 carries.
  • Defense slams the door: Mentor was held to 67 rushing yards and never reached the end zone for the first time all year (Kee On Sports). Quarterback Brogan Jones went 12-of-24 for 137 yards with two interceptions — Kelley’s pick-six, and Landon Lehmer’s seventh interception of the season, returned 56 yards to set up Neal’s touchdown. Mentor’s only points were Danny Kristo’s 45-yard field goal in the first quarter.
  • History: This is St. Edward’s sixth consecutive Region 1 championship, and the third straight year the Eagles have beaten Mentor in this exact game to win it. The Eagles advance to their 16th state semifinal appearance (Columbus Dispatch), chasing the program’s eighth state title and first since 2023.
  • One concern: Starting offensive lineman John Marquard was ejected in the second quarter after a second personal foul (Kee On Sports) — worth watching for his availability for the semifinal.

2. Scouting report: the state semifinal — Pickerington North Panthers (11–3)

OHSAA Division I State Semifinal — Friday, November 27, 7:07 p.m. kickoff
Woody Hayes Quaker Stadium, New Philadelphia High School, 131 C. William Bill Kidd Dr., New Philadelphia, OH 44663
Watch: Spectrum News 1 · Stream: OHSAA.tv ($9.99, OHSAA event) · Listen: free audio via the St. Edward athletics site · Tickets: through OHSAA / Hometown Ticketing — school season and Holy Cross passes are not valid (stedwardeagles.com).
Stakes: Winner plays Cincinnati Princeton (13–0) or Springfield (11–2) in the Division I state final, Friday, December 4 at 7:30 p.m. at Tom Benson Hall of Fame Stadium in Canton (OHSAA).

How North got here

  • First regional title since 2012. The No. 6 seed Panthers won Region 3 — the program’s first regional championship in coach Nate Hillerich’s 10 seasons, and only its second state semifinal appearance ever, the first in 14 years (Columbus Dispatch). North lost its only previous semifinal, 26–21 to eventual champion Cincinnati Moeller in 2012.
  • Playoff path: 37–0 over Groveport Madison, 45–7 at Worthington Kilbourne (four touchdown passes from Demetrius Neal-Nesbitt), 14–10 at Upper Arlington, then the Region 3 final: 17–14 over defending champion Olentangy Liberty on Nov 20.
  • The Liberty comeback tells you who they are: North led 9–0 at halftime on three Niko Agganis field goals (26, 34 and 21 yards), then gave up two touchdowns in the first 36 seconds of the fourth quarter off fumbles to trail 14–9. The Panthers answered anyway: with 4:34 left, Neal-Nesbitt found Noah Dumas over the middle on third-and-11 for a 16-yard touchdown, Dennis White added the two-point conversion, and Andyriq Thomas ran for 14 yards on third-and-7 to run out the clock (Dispatch). Battle-tested and resilient — North’s three regular-season losses came by a combined six points (Dispatch).

Key Panthers to watch

  • QB Demetrius Neal-Nesbitt (sophomore): 135-of-220 for 2,023 yards, 23 touchdowns and just 4 interceptions on the season (Dispatch). Threw for 179–191 yards in the Liberty final (sources vary slightly) including the game-winner. Only a sophomore, but he does not play like one in big moments.
  • WR Kymani Johnson: 41 catches for 712 yards and 8 touchdowns (Dispatch) — North’s big-play threat; his 19-yard catch set up the winning score against Liberty.
  • WR Noah Dumas (senior): The clutch target — 7 catches for 55 yards and the winning touchdown vs. Liberty, plus the long go-ahead score vs. Upper Arlington in the semifinal round.
  • RB Andyriq Thomas: 785 rushing yards and 8 touchdowns (Dispatch); the closer who sealed both the Upper Arlington and Liberty wins on the ground.
  • K Niko Agganis: Three field goals in one half of a regional final — in a tight, low-scoring game, North trusts its kicking game.

Style, strengths — and where the Eagles can attack

  • Strengths: A balanced offense behind an efficient young quarterback, a defense that held Liberty scoreless for three quarters and Upper Arlington to 10 points, and a team that has won four straight playoff games, three of them away from home. Hillerich on St. Edward: “There’s really no weakness… they just reload” (Dispatch).
  • Weaknesses to test: Ball security — two fumbles nearly cost North the Liberty game, turning a 9–0 lead into a deficit in 36 seconds. And North has not solved St. Edward lately: the Eagles shut the Panthers out in the season openers in both 2024 (28–0) and 2025 (24–0) (Dispatch).
  • What the Eagles need to do: (1) Start fast again, as they did against Mentor — North is comfortable in close, late games, so do not let this one stay close. (2) Pressure Neal-Nesbitt and take away Johnson deep; make a sophomore quarterback drive the long field against this defense. (3) Win the run game both ways — Lucas, Neal and Woods against Thomas — and (4) protect the ball and the kicking game, because North’s kicker and defense are built to steal a low-scoring game.
  • The pick: The Columbus Dispatch predictions panel picked St. Edward unanimously.
