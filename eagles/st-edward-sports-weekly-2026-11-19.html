St. Edward Eagles Sports Weekly

Issue No. 2 · Week of November 14–19, 2026 · Lakewood, Ohio · All times Eastern

1. Top story: Eagles rout Hudson 38–14, back in the regional final

Final — OHSAA Division I, Region 1 semifinal (Fri, Nov 13): St. Edward 38, Hudson 14
Quarter by quarter: St. Edward 10–14–7–7; Hudson 0–7–0–7
  • 24–0 before Hudson could blink: The No. 1 seed Eagles scored the game’s first 24 points at First Federal Lakewood Stadium, cashing in on a short field all night. Hudson hurt itself on special teams — three errant punt snaps, one leading to a block, plus a muffed kickoff — and St. Edward pounced on nearly every mistake (Akron Beacon Journal).
  • Ronnie Neal carries the load: Neal ran for three touchdowns — from 3 yards on the opening drive, again in the second quarter, and a 5-yard score to cap a 98-yard fourth-quarter drive. The Beacon Journal credited him with 139 yards on 19 carries; MaxPreps’ recap had 140 yards and three scores. (Kee On Sports had a lower rushing total, 121 yards — as in Issue No. 1, the official/Beacon and MaxPreps lines are used here where recaps conflict.)
  • Lucas adds two: Quarterback Langston Lucas plunged in for two 1-yard touchdown runs — one set up by the blocked punt at the Hudson 1 late in the second quarter, the other midway through the third to make it 31–7.
  • Defense bottles up a star: The Eagles held Hudson’s All-State running back Reno Ferri to 43 yards on 13 carries (Kee On Sports) and limited Hudson to 5 completions in 17 pass attempts. Landon Lehmer’s leaping first-quarter interception at the Hudson 30 set up Dallas Nixon’s 33-yard field goal and a 10–0 lead.
  • Late Hudson scores: QB Hayden Lipinski ran 8 yards for a touchdown six seconds before halftime, then left early in the third quarter with a foot injury. Backup Ben Hegedish hit Ferri on a 33-yard screen for Hudson’s final score with 2:37 left. Hudson finishes 9–4.
  • Seven straight: The win is St. Edward’s seventh in a row and moves the Eagles to 11–1 and into the Division I, Region 1 final for a rematch with Mentor — the team St. Edward knocked out of the playoffs in both 2024 and 2025.

2. Tomorrow night: Regional final — St. Edward vs. Mentor

  • Kickoff: Friday, November 20, 7:07 PM at Byers Field at Robert M. Boulton Stadium in Parma. No. 1 seed St. Edward (11–1) meets No. 3 seed Mentor (10–2), which survived Canton McKinley 16–13 in the other semifinal.
  • Watch / listen: Televised on Spectrum News 1; stream on OHSAA.tv ($9.99, an OHSAA event); free audio stream via the St. Edward Athletics site. Tickets through the OHSAA website — school season passes and Holy Cross passes are not valid for playoff games.
  • What’s at stake: The winner is the Region 1 champion and advances to the Division I state semifinals on Friday, November 27, against the Region 3 champion — either Olentangy Liberty or Pickerington North, who meet Friday in Columbus. The OHSAA announced that semifinal pairing on November 18; the semifinal site will be announced Sunday, November 22. The state final is Friday, December 4, 7:30 PM at Tom Benson Hall of Fame Stadium in Canton.
  • Key matchup: Mentor has reached the Region 1 final for the third straight year — and St. Edward has ended the Cardinals’ season the last two times. The Eagles’ formula from the Hudson game travels well: a heavy ground game behind Neal and Lucas, field position, and a defense that just held one of Ohio’s best backs to 43 yards.

2A. Scouting report: Know the Cardinals

Mentor is no stranger to this game — and no stranger to heartbreak in it. This is the third straight year the Cardinals have reached the Region 1 final, and St. Edward has sent them home the last two times. Here is the deep intel on the team standing between the Eagles and a sixth straight regional title.

How Mentor got here

  • Record: 10–2, No. 3 seed, out of the Greater Cleveland Conference. The only losses: a 51–7 blowout at St. Ignatius in Week 3, and a 56–30 loss at Strongsville on October 2 — a game senior quarterback Brogan Jones missed (Ohio Varsity). Mentor has won five straight since.
  • Playoff path: A first-round bye, then payback — a 45–14 rout of Strongsville in the quarterfinal rematch — then last week’s 16–13 survival against Canton McKinley, sealed by a goal-line stand at the 1-yard line as the first half expired and an onside-kick recovery in the final two minutes (Canton Repository).
  • Full season line: Olmsted Falls W 38–17, Erie McDowell W 31–30, at St. Ignatius L 7–51, Brunswick W 63–18, at Riverside W 35–14, Cleveland Heights W 49–14, at Strongsville L 30–56, at Shaker Heights W 42–3, Lorain W by forfeit, at Euclid W 31–0, Strongsville W 45–14, Canton McKinley W 16–13 (MaxPreps).

The offense: two quarterbacks, big plays, 37 points a night

  • Scoring machine — usually: Mentor averages 37.1 points per game and topped 30 in every game this season except last week’s 16 against McKinley — just the second time all year the Cardinals were held under 30 (Canton Repository).
  • Senior QB Brogan Jones: The starter and a team captain. Entering the McKinley game he had completed 53.5% of his passes for 1,249 yards with 8 touchdowns — but 9 interceptions — plus 2 rushing scores (Canton Repository). He threw the 35-yard touchdown to Jeremiah Claridy that gave Mentor its only first-half points last week. Jones also has history in this round: he ran in the game-winning two-point conversion against McKinley in last year’s semifinal.
  • Sophomore QB Corbin Bissett: The change-up, and a real running threat — 561 passing yards, 7 passing touchdowns and 3 interceptions, plus 7 rushing touchdowns entering last week (Canton Repository). He ran for three scores and threw for another in the Strongsville rematch, and his first-down run let Mentor run out the clock against McKinley. The Eagles must account for his legs whenever he rotates in.
  • RB Ryan Orsog: The senior lead back since Braylon Callahan’s season-ending injury. Orsog entered last week with a team-high 502 rushing yards at 6.3 yards per carry and 5 touchdowns, then scored the go-ahead 6-yard touchdown with 5:07 left against McKinley. His yardage total for that game is disputed — the Canton Repository credited him with 92 yards on 14 carries, while Ohio Varsity had 105 yards — so, as with the Hudson rushing lines in Section 1, both figures are shown rather than averaged.
  • The Callahan loss: Mentor’s run game took a real hit when Braylon Callahan was lost for the season. In just six games he had piled up 681 rushing yards at 9.3 yards per carry with 14 touchdowns (MaxPreps figures via Ohio Varsity). Orsog is productive, but the explosive home-run back is gone.
  • Receivers: Senior Logan Berwald is the top target — team highs of 40 catches for 692 yards and 5 touchdowns entering last week. Junior Jeremiah Claridy also has 5 touchdown catches and burned McKinley on the 35-yarder (Canton Repository). Mentor has produced touchdown passes of 58, 64 and 80 yards this season, so the deep ball is a genuine threat (Ohio Varsity).
  • The line: Mentor replaced all five offensive linemen from last season, including three Division I signees, yet coach Matt Gray leaned on the new group to close out McKinley — the Cardinals’ final touchdown drive was five straight runs. “Our offensive line, they imposed their will,” Gray said afterward (Canton Repository).
  • Kicker Daniel Kristo: Normally automatic — he had made 47 straight extra points before missing his first in 48 tries last week, and he is 6-for-8 on field goals with a long of 49, plus a 50-yarder at Strongsville (Canton Repository; Virtual Box Score). But McKinley blocked his 34-yard attempt, and his 20-yarder in the fourth quarter is what finally put Mentor in front.

The defense: the story of Mentor’s turnaround

  • Night and day: In the first Strongsville meeting, Mentor’s defense was gashed for 332 rushing yards on just 20 carries in a 56–30 loss (Virtual Box Score). Since then the Cardinals have allowed 3, 0, 14 and 13 points in their last four games — including shutting out Euclid and holding that same Strongsville team to 14 in the rematch.
  • Disciplined: Mentor was not penalized a single time against McKinley, while the Bulldogs were flagged 10 times for 68 yards (Canton Repository).
  • But a dual-threat QB hurt them: McKinley’s Braylon Crook threw for 200 yards and ran for 100 more with two rushing touchdowns, repeatedly escaping on third and fourth down. That is the template Langston Lucas — who ran for two scores of his own against Hudson — will try to copy.

Where the Eagles can attack

  • Make Jones throw into coverage: Nine interceptions against eight touchdown passes (Canton Repository) is the biggest number on the scouting report. The Eagles’ secondary decided last year’s final — Makhi Boone’s pick-six and a second Boone interception sealed the 27–7 win (Kee On Sports) — and Landon Lehmer’s leaping interception set the tone against Hudson.
  • Run right at them: The one defense-wide collapse on Mentor’s film came against a power running team (Strongsville’s 332 rushing yards). St. Edward’s formula — Ronnie Neal (three touchdowns vs. Hudson) behind a heavy ground game — is exactly that kind of offense.
  • Contain the offense early: McKinley proved Mentor can be bottled up, holding the Cardinals to just 217 total yards (Canton Repository). A fast start like the 24–0 jump on Hudson forces Mentor’s two-quarterback rotation to play from behind.
  • Win special teams: Hudson’s punt-snap meltdown handed the Eagles last week’s game, and Mentor showed cracks of its own — a blocked field goal and a missed extra point against McKinley. Pressure on Kristo and the punt team is worth scheming for.

Recent history: same game, same field, same result

  • 2025 regional final (Byers Field): St. Edward 27, Mentor 7. The Cardinals arrived undefeated at 12–0; the Eagles’ defense dominated, Boone’s pick-six put it away, and St. Edward lifted its fifth straight Region 1 trophy (Kee On Sports; Spectrum News 1).
  • 2024 regional final: St. Edward 13, Mentor 7. A rain-soaked defensive slugfest — Brad Eaton’s touchdown runs of 80 and 67 yards were the Eagles’ offense, and a late interception sealed it as Mentor was held to its lowest point total of that season (The Edsman; Sports Illustrated).
  • The pick: The Columbus Dispatch’s predictions panel took St. Edward unanimously in this game. The Eagles are favored — but note that Mentor’s last four opponents have scored 3, 0, 14 and 13. Nothing about Friday will be easy.
