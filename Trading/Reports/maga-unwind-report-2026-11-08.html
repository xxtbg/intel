Post-Midterm Market Report

Sunday, November 8, 2026. Prices: Hyperliquid, ~8:10 AM ET.

1. Lame-duck Iran escalation risk

  • Hormuz — new incidents today: Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy says three oil tankers suffered explosions and severe fires in the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday after using a route it had declared off-limits; the ships' identities and crew status remain independently unverified. CENTCOM says its new Task Force Iron Gate will target Iranian supply routes over land and across the Caspian Sea. Qatar's prime minister says Doha is still mediating; no agreement has been announced. (The Levant Files live coverage, Nov 8)
  • Qatari plan in detail: Qatar is pressing Tehran to allow UN/IAEA inspectors back into its bombed nuclear sites, including those holding highly enriched uranium. In return the US would immediately lift the naval blockade and release part of Iran's frozen $8bn. Iran would open the strait on day two via a route already negotiated with Oman. Iran's other preconditions — ending the war in Lebanon, US withdrawal from its coastline, sanctions relief, war reparations — remain the sticking point. Iran sent a nuclear proposal via Qatar on Thursday, but its security chief says Iran will neither open Hormuz nor negotiate until its reported seven conditions are met.
  • Odds still lean escalation over deal: Eurasia Group puts renewed US strikes at 60%, continued standoff at 30%, and a deal at 10%. Two US carrier groups are already in the region, per US officials cited by CBS. The largest group in an ICIS poll of traders expects Trump to escalate; ICIS's own energy director told Bloomberg a deal is likelier. Trump on Thursday: the US is "locked and loaded" if needed — "You will know soon enough."
  • The window and the purse: the Republican-controlled 119th Congress sits until the 120th seats on January 3, 2027 — roughly nine weeks of unilateral Iran authority. Incoming Speaker Jeffries lists ending the war as a top priority and incoming Senate Leader Schumer says the path runs through spending: "We are going to end the war." First test comes earlier: federal funding runs out December 11, spending bills need 60 Senate votes, and the Pentagon wants another $67bn for the war.
  • Oil: Brent settled Friday at $98.32, down more than 16% on the week, and WTI at $90.83, down nearly 13% — markets betting the midterm result opens a path to a US-Iran deal. No deal exists yet and tanker fires continue. On Hyperliquid today: BRENTOIL $97.32 (24h +0.3%, 7d -13.0%), open interest 4.03M units (~$392M); CL $90.41 (24h +0.6%, 7d -11.9%). The war premium has been cut hard; position size in Brent has not left.

2. DEX and privacy-coin inflows — regulatory and sanctions risk

  • MEXC ban — clock not yet started: FinCEN's Nov 6 order under Operation Economic Outcast cuts MEXC Global off from US dollar rails over at least $16B in illicit flows (May 2021–May 2026) tied to Iran, Russia and North Korea, including ~$1.5B in Russian sanctions evasion and ~$22M linked to Iran's Central Bank and the IRGC Qods Force. It takes effect 30 days after Federal Register publication — and as of Friday evening the Register's public index still showed no MEXC document, so the 30-day clock had not started. Treasury also designated eight Canadian money services businesses accused of enabling Iranian networks the same day. MEXC says it is operating normally and user assets are secure; OKX has warned users against direct transfers from MEXC.
  • CLARITY Act still dead in the lame duck: no new text or vote since Nov 5 reporting that House Democrats are drafting their own market-structure bill — stricter DeFi rules than the BRCA, more jurisdiction shifted from the CFTC to the SEC, Waters-style consumer protections, and ethics curbs on officials' crypto holdings. Elizabeth Warren is set to chair Senate Banking. DeFi remains the segment with most to lose in the rewrite.
  • Privacy coins — mixed tape, structural bid intact: the privacy sector's market cap was $43.9B on Nov 8 (CoinPaprika), with ZEC the largest name. Earlier in the week ZEC's slide from above $1,250 triggered $14.55M in liquidations, over 85% of them longs, while XMR rose nearly 3% and extended its 90-day gain above 75%. Zcash's NU7 upgrade (Nov 5) froze about $16M left in the legacy Sprout pool; a recovery proposal exists but is not approved. Longer-dated overhang: the EU Anti-Money Laundering Regulation bars regulated providers from anonymity-enhancing coins from July 10, 2027 — self-custody is not banned in the US or EU.
  • Price action today: XMR $604.58 (24h -1.5%, 7d +0.3%, day volume $38M); ZEC $1,275.00 (24h +0.2%, 7d +2.9%, day volume $215M — the largest crypto volume on this watchlist). DEX tokens still do not confirm the inflow thesis: HYPE $77.66 (24h +0.4%, 7d -11.4%, volume $124M) and RUNE $0.582 (24h -2.1%, 7d -4.2%, volume only $0.4M, funding negative).
  • Arbitrum (added Nov 7): ARB $0.1698 (24h +1.7%, 7d +2.2%), open interest 88.0M ARB (~$15M), day volume $4.2M. Linkage note: Hyperliquid's matching runs on its own L1 (HyperCore); Arbitrum is the bridge chain — USDC deposits and withdrawals route through Arbitrum, which is why ARB is monitored alongside it.

3. Musk companies — political risk premium

  • Premium persists in the tape, no new congressional action yet: TSLA (Hyperliquid perp) $357.67 (24h -0.3%, 7d -10.5%); SPCX (perp) $135.49 (24h +0.4%, 7d -1.6%). Note: these are Hyperliquid perp marks on a Sunday — the US cash market is closed, so treat levels as indicative until Monday's open.
  • Context from the week: on Wednesday both names fell ~5.5% premarket while S&P futures rose over 1%. XTB's Kathleen Brooks: "There is now a political risk premium attached to both Tesla and SpaceX," mainly via scrutiny of government contracts and slowing of the possible Tesla-SpaceX merger — Congress cannot block a merger but can slow it with extra scrutiny. House Democrats have signaled subpoenas and inquiries into Musk's DOGE role and federal dealings once they hold gavels from Jan 3.
  • Read bear commentary as commentary: one widely shared Nov 6 piece claimed TSLA fell 11% on Nov 5 and SPCX dropped below its $135 IPO price, framing a fading "political moat." That is a market theory in circulation, not an established fact — no regulator, committee or court has acted against either company, and earlier consensus was Moderate Buy (TSLA) and Strong Buy (SPCX). Texas adds a second front: a new Democratic governor-elect opposed to subsidies and data-center expansion raises state-level risk for Gigafactory Austin, robotaxi pilots and Starbase.

Restructuring and macro — what else moved the picture

  • Rotation is real but not one-way: Nov 4 trading lifted healthcare and clean energy — Centene +12.6%, Nextpower +11.1% — while defense contractors and smaller data-center operators came under pressure and Microsoft, Amazon and Alphabet gained over 6% (Reuters via Zacks). A separate gridlock playbook rotates out of megacaps into bipartisan defense suppliers (Heico, TransDigm), domestic energy and market infrastructure (Cboe, Tradeweb), arguing gridlock makes defense spending and IRA manufacturing credits harder to cut. The two takes conflict on defense; both agree policy-sensitive sectors are being repriced, not the whole index.
  • Jobs beat, hike odds up: October payrolls rose 165,000 against ~65,000 expected; unemployment 4.1%; average hourly earnings +0.4% on the month. December Fed hike odds rose to roughly 77–80%. The Fed held at 3.75–4.00% on Oct 28 (two officials wanted a hike); next FOMC is Dec 8–9. The S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,376.86, down about 2.7% on the week.
  • Next data test — CPI Tuesday: October CPI is due Tuesday, Nov 10 at 8:30 AM ET — consensus +0.3% headline, +0.2% core month-on-month, with no year-on-year figure because October 2025 data was never collected during the 2025 shutdown. Last week's oil drop will not show in October prices. Also this week: EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook (Tue Nov 10), OPEC Monthly Oil Market Report (Wed Nov 11), PPI (Fri Nov 13), OPEC+ ministerial (Nov 29).
