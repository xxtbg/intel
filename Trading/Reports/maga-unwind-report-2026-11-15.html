Post-Midterm Market Report

Sunday, November 15, 2026. Prices: Hyperliquid, ~5:30 AM ET. *TSLA/SPCX figures are Hyperliquid perp marks; US cash market is closed Sunday.

1. Lame-duck Iran escalation risk

  • New Hormuz mine claims Sat — unverified: Iran's navy/IRGC said Sat Nov 14 three oil tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines in Hormuz on unauthorized routes (one morning, two evening); some IRGC tallies say five vessels in 24h. Reuters could not verify, no names/flags/casualties given, and CENTCOM dismissed the claims as false. Fourth round of such claims in six weeks, none independently confirmed. UN: 25 seafarers killed around Hormuz since Feb.
  • Strategy still blockade, not strikes: Trump has waved away needing a deal ("we've got their money"), Rubio says no need for military action while sanctions bite, and renewed strikes were called "quite unlikely" after the Qatar PM meeting Nov 9. Status quo: naval blockade (CENTCOM: >$25B Iranian crude blocked) plus new Task Force Iron Gate on land/Caspian routes, a third carrier moving to the region, and occasional tit-for-tat strikes — Iran said Fri the US hit an island radar/drone site in Hormuz, Iran hit back at a US radar in Bahrain. Iran offers a short pause on ship attacks only if its seven demands are met (end blockade/sanctions, unfreeze funds, ceasefires in Lebanon/Gaza) — Rubio calls them unrealistic.
  • Oil down a second week despite the claims: Brent settled Fri $95.84 (+$0.02), WTI $88.56 (-0.2%), both ~-2.5% on the week. IEA Nov 13: market back in surplus for the first time since the war began Feb 28 — Q4 supply ~105.4 mb/d (+2.3 mb/d in Oct), ~1.7 mb/d above demand, Gulf exports recovered (74–82% of pre-war in different IEA cuts), 2026 supply estimate raised 920k b/d. EIA: US stocks +4.7M bbl (week ended Nov 6), US output a record 14.013 mb/d. Weekend mine claims put upside risk into the Sun evening reopen ($98 bull / $91 bear reference levels), but the surplus has not changed.

2. DEX / privacy inflows on regulatory & sanctions risk

  • MEXC cutoff dated Dec 10, de-risking spreading: FinCEN's Nov 6 order under Operation Economic Outcast (≥$16B / $16.7B illicit flows May 2021–May 2026, incl. ~$1.5B Russian sanctions evasion and ~$1B+ with 40+ Iranian exchanges, plus IRGC-QF and North Korea links) was published in the Federal Register Nov 10, so the US financial-institution cutoff takes effect Dec 10, 2026, no expiration. It covers crypto/stablecoin transfers, not just wires. Binance (Nov 10) told users to stop MEXC transfers, KuCoin may withhold MEXC deposits for compliance review (users report up to 30 days) and declines withdrawals to MEXC, and OKX warned MEXC-sourced funds may be frozen — offshore venues are closing routes ahead of the deadline.
  • Market structure still SEC-ward, no text yet: No new bill text this week. The standing picture: CLARITY is dead in the lame duck (failed Senate cloture 49–50 in Sept), House Democrats are drafting their own bill for the Jan 3 Congress — stricter DeFi than the BRCA, more SEC / less CFTC, Waters consumer protections, ethics curbs on officials' crypto. That direction supports the DEX/privacy thesis on paper.
  • Prices say the bid is unwinding, not building: Privacy sector cap fell from $44.6B (Nov 1) to ~$37.6B (Nov 12). On Hyperliquid over 7d: ZEC -29.0%, XMR -17.0%, HYPE -12.0% — profit-taking/liquidation after the October run, not fresh inflows. Volume is still heavy (ZEC $573.8M/day, HYPE $1.26B/day on Hyperliquid). Hyperliquid itself did $10.08B perp volume in 24h to Nov 14 (48% of tracked onchain perp DEX volume, $6.18B OI) — the venue share story holds even as its token falls.

3. Musk companies — political risk premium

  • Premium narrative now in print, recovery in price: Motley Fool (Nov 14) recaps TSLA/SPCX losing >$420B combined in two days post-midterm on probe fears, then recovering most of it to near pre-election levels. Hyperliquid 7d agrees on recovery: TSLA +8.2%, SPCX +2.0% — the premium is fading in price while the overhang remains.
  • Exposure channels unchanged: Possible congressional probes (Cybercab/FSD scrutiny, Starlink/Starshield access for Ukraine, federal dependence on SpaceX), data-center backlash (both need large AI compute; Texas elected a Democratic AG/governor slate for the first time since 1994, with a proposed pause on new data centers — Terafab named), and a Tesla–SpaceX merger that would "certainly attract even more attention" / regulatory pushback under the incoming Congress. Musk's counter: Tesla has no government contracts, SpaceX government revenue <10% and falling below 5% within a year; Deutsche Bank / Morgan Stanley / UBS have called dips/the AI selloff overdone.
  • Note: "Collapse" remains a market theory in commentary, not an established fact. No hearing, subpoena, or merger filing was reported this week; the new Congress is not seated until Jan 3, 2027.

4. Restructuring / rotation & macro

  • S&P's best week since August, on a hike path: S&P 500 +2.51% on the week to 7,561.67 (Fri -0.40%), snapping a four-week losing streak; Nasdaq +2.27%, Dow +2.07%, semis +8.05% despite Nvidia -12.75% after CEO Jensen Huang's resignation Nov 12. October CPI +0.4% m/m (vs 0.3% expected), PPI +6.5% y/y (largest since Nov 2022) — CME FedWatch prices a Dec 9 hike as fully locked in (~10% odds of 50bp; Waller has put 50bp on the table). 10y yield 5.14–5.15%, 2y ~4.86%. Gridlock relief + earnings drove the week; rates are the constraint.
  • Rotation pattern intact, no new hard flow data this week: The post-midterm map from Nov 4–5 still frames positioning — consumer +4.9%, healthcare +4.2% (HCA +10.3% at the Nov 4 close), clean energy (First Solar +4.6%, Enphase +3.7%) bid on coverage/incentive hopes, energy -5% that day and defense/AI-data-center names under scrutiny risk. Treat as a framework awaiting confirmation from funded provisions, not a completed restructuring.
  • Gold softer with the dollar bid: GOLD perp -5.5% over 7d on Hyperliquid as the dollar index hit its highest since Apr 2025 on faster-hike bets — not included in today's table (no direct theory link), noted here only as macro context.
