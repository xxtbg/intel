Post-Midterm Market Report

Monday, November 9, 2026. Prices: Hyperliquid, ~6:20 AM ET.

1. Lame-duck Iran escalation risk

  • Trump dials back strike talk: in a weekend CNN interview, Trump said fresh US strikes on Iran are highly unlikely as long as talks on the nuclear program and reopening Hormuz keep progressing. Brent fell about 2% Monday to $96.29 on the comments. Iran's foreign minister Araghchi says the talks have made no progress, and Secretary of State Rubio calls Iran's demands unrealistic — the two sides are describing different negotiations. (Reuters via TBS News / Devdiscourse, Nov 9; CoinCentral, Nov 9)
  • Hormuz still burning: Iranian media say no ships crossed the strait on Sunday. UKMTO verified an outbound tanker struck by an unidentified projectile 15 km northwest of Khasab, Oman, and a second tanker caught fire nearby with its crew evacuated; Iran claims tankers hit naval mines in unauthorized southern lanes, unconfirmed by maritime authorities. On Saturday a US aircraft disabled the bulk carrier Hoda in the Gulf of Oman for defying the naval blockade, and CENTCOM's new Task Force Iron Gate is targeting Iranian supply routes. Three US carrier groups are in theater. (Splash247, Nov 9; Region Alert, Nov 9)
  • Qatari mediation reaches the White House: Qatar's prime minister arrived at the White House on Monday to present draft ceasefire terms. The Qatari plan reported last week — IAEA inspectors back in, blockade lifted, part of Iran's frozen $8bn released, strait reopened via the Oman route — still runs into Iran's other preconditions. No agreement announced. (Region Alert, Nov 9)
  • Oil tape: the deal bet keeps unwinding the war premium even as tankers burn. Hyperliquid: BRENTOIL $95.14 (24h -2.2%, 7d -12.4%), open interest still 3.63M units (~$345M) — size has not left; CL $88.58 (24h -2.1%, 7d -11.1%). Brent is now below the $93–$105 reference band's midpoint and testing its lower edge.
  • The window: the Republican-controlled 119th Congress sits until the 120th seats on January 3, 2027. First hard test before that: federal funding runs out December 11, and the Pentagon wants another $67bn for the war. G7 foreign ministers meet in Paris Monday — allies' first read on whether the midterm defeat changes the administration's Iran approach. (Saxo, Nov 9)

2. DEX and privacy-coin inflows — regulatory and sanctions risk

  • MEXC ban — clock still not started: FinCEN's Nov 6 order under Operation Economic Outcast (Section 9714) bars US institutions from any fund transmittals to or from MEXC Global over at least $16B in illicit flows (May 2021–May 2026), including ~$1.5B in Russian sanctions evasion and ~$1B with 40+ Iranian exchanges. As of Nov 8–9 reporting the order has been submitted to the Federal Register but not yet published — the PDF still carries a placeholder date — so the 30-day clock has not started and there is no effective date. First visible exit: NatGold Digital, a Florida gold-token issuer, left MEXC on its own within a day of the order. MEXC says it is operating normally and has opened an independent review of its AML/sanctions controls. (LeapRate, Nov 9; CryptoNews, Nov 8; CoinDesk, Nov 7)
  • One deregulatory counter-signal: FinCEN and the Fed have withdrawn the 2020 proposal to cut the cross-border Travel Rule recordkeeping threshold from $3,000 to $250; the $3,000 threshold stays, with the withdrawal effective Nov 9. Enforcement is targeted at named bad actors, not broadened reporting — for now. (Cryptoweek, Nov 7)
  • Market structure: no new text or vote on the House Democrats' SEC-led alternative to the CLARITY Act — still drafting, built on Waters' investor-protection amendment and pre-failure Senate Democratic provisions, with stricter DeFi rules expected. DeFi remains the segment with most to lose.
  • Privacy coins soften: sector market cap $42.94B on Nov 9, 24h volume $1.35B, only 2 of 21 tracked coins rising (CoinPaprika). ZEC $1,222.60 spot (-3.3% 24h, -2.3% 7d); XMR $597.71 (-0.4% 24h, -1.5% 7d). The structural bid from last week is pausing, not reversing — XMR is holding up better than ZEC.
  • Hyperliquid tape — heavy selling, DEX still not confirming: ZEC $1,203.20 (24h -5.3%, 7d -3.8%, day volume $448M); XMR $593.27 (24h -1.5%, 7d -2.6%, volume $31M); HYPE $72.85 (24h -5.5%, 7d -15.2%, volume $499M — the heaviest on this list). Selling is broad (BTC -2.4% 24h), so today's privacy/DEX weakness reads as risk-off, not thesis failure — but the inflow thesis still has no DEX confirmation.
  • ARB bucks the tape: ARB $0.1772 (24h +4.5%, 7d +11.6%), open interest 85.8M ARB (~$15M), volume $8.6M. Arbitrum is Hyperliquid's USDC bridge chain, so it is monitored alongside the DEX complex; it is the only crypto name here green on both 24h and 7d while the rest sell off.

3. Musk companies — political risk premium

  • Musk pushes back: Musk said he would happily cancel any SpaceX government contract the government no longer wants — "Just say the word" — arguing the contracts are the best product at the lowest price. He put government work at under 10% of SpaceX revenue this quarter and under 5% within a year, and noted Tesla holds no government contracts. (BigGo Finance, Nov 9)
  • A bank calls the selloff overdone: Deutsche Bank (analyst Edison Yu, Nov 6 note) reiterated Buy on SpaceX with a $250 target — roughly 84% upside from the Nov 5 close of $123.62 — calling the post-election pullback a buying opportunity. The bank's most concrete near-term risk is not Congress but Texas: tighter data-center scrutiny from the incoming governor, with most SpaceX compute expected to sit outside Texas. SpaceX is down ~17% year-to-date and Tesla ~20% on the figures cited. (BigGo Finance, Nov 9)
  • Monday is the first real cash-market test: Hyperliquid perps pre-open: TSLA $357.64 (24h flat, 7d -11.3%, volume $24M); SPCX $135.44 (24h +0.2%, 7d -1.7%, volume $62M — heavy for this name). US cash trading opens 9:30 AM ET; whether the premium widens or the Deutsche Bank view wins shows up today. Note: TSLA/SPCX figures here are Hyperliquid perp marks taken before the cash open.
  • Still no action, only pricing: no regulator, committee or court has acted against either company. The premium rests on signaled House inquiries into the DOGE role and federal contracts from Jan 3, merger scrutiny Congress can slow but not block, and Texas state-level risk to Gigafactory Austin, robotaxi pilots and Starbase. "Collapse" framing in circulation remains a market theory, not an established fact.
