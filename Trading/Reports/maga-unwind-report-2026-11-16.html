Post-Midterm Market Report

Monday, November 16, 2026. Prices: Hyperliquid, ~5:30 AM ET. *TSLA/SPCX figures are Hyperliquid perp marks; US cash market was closed over the weekend (last cash closes Fri Nov 13).

1. Lame-duck Iran escalation risk

  • Strike pause is conditional, not a settlement: Reuters (Nov 14) reports Trump told senior advisers he may hold off on renewed strikes as long as Iran or its Houthi proxies avoid a new provocation; no final decision. That follows his Oct 31 remark to Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman that he planned to strike after the midterms — MBS urged him to let sanctions grind instead. Public line stays mixed: Thu Nov 12 Trump said the US no longer needs a deal and would hit Iran "without hesitation" if needed; Fri Nov 13 Rubio told the Federalist Society the war is "militarily effectively over." Blockade and sanctions pressure continue either way.
  • New Hormuz mine claim Sun night — still unverified: Iran's navy said Sun night (Nov 15) an oil tanker caught fire after striking a naval mine east of the Strait of Hormuz (Reuters, Nov 16), following Fri claims of two tankers. No vessel name/flag/casualties independently confirmed; CENTCOM has dismissed earlier rounds of these claims as false. Iran warned at the UN Security Council Fri of an "immediate and decisive response" to any renewed US or Israeli attack.
  • Oil fell again Mon despite the claim: Early Mon Nov 16 Reuters pricing: Brent $95.30 (-0.6%), WTI $88.34 (-0.3%). IEA (Fri Nov 13): Q4 returns to surplus for the first time since the war began — supply ~105 mb/d, ~1.7 mb/d above demand, Hormuz shipments nearly restored to pre-war levels, plus 71M bbl of emergency-reserve releases still to come by end-Feb. Chinese refinery throughput fell in Oct, softening demand. The escalation premium keeps leaking out; a verified tanker loss is the trigger that would put it back.

2. DEX / privacy inflows on regulatory & sanctions risk

  • MEXC cutoff Dec 10 — no new order, clock running: FinCEN's Nov 6 order (Operation Economic Outcast; $16.7B likely illicit flows May 2021–May 2026, incl. Russian sanctions evasion and Garantex/Grinex links) was published Nov 10 (91 FR 72420), so US financial institutions must stop all transfers to/from MEXC Global — crypto included — from Dec 10, 2026, with no cessation date. Binance, OKX and Gate are already holding or blocking MEXC transfers ahead of the deadline, and the order's taint follows coins even through a personal wallet.
  • Market structure: no new text: CLARITY remains dead in the lame duck (Senate cloture failed 49–50 in Sept; Paradigm's Slaughter, Nov 5). The House Democrats' own bill for the Jan 3 Congress is still only reported outlines: stricter DeFi rules than the BRCA, more jurisdiction to the SEC vs CFTC, Waters consumer protections, ethics curbs on officials' crypto. Direction still supports the DEX/privacy thesis on paper; nothing new to price this weekend.
  • Privacy unwind deepened, venue share held: Hyperliquid 7d: ZEC -26.1%, XMR -17.8%, RUNE -11.1%, HYPE -8.1% — all red again Mon morning. ZEC mechanics (Kairos/CoinGlass via AInvest, Nov 14): the Nov 10 break traced to a single ~$15M leveraged long closed in seconds, ~$18M liquidations in 24h (72% longs), perp open interest ~13% of ZEC market value on Nov 12 with funding still positive — a crowded long unwind, not a privacy-story failure. Venue side: DefiLlama puts Hyperliquid perp volume at $9.74B in 24h to Nov 16, largest of $23.5B total perp DEX volume — flow share holds even as token prices fall.

3. Musk companies — political risk premium

  • No subpoena yet — preparation, with a Nov 30 deadline: A Nov 16 audit of 18 sources finds no subpoena issued, no charge, no finding: Sen. Coons has a "list of questions for Musk when I subpoena him" (Starshield, Putin remarks), and Sens. Shaheen and Coons sent a preservation letter over Starshield restrictions on Ukraine service (Oct 13–24 and again Nov 12), demanding records back to Sep 1 and a response by Nov 30. Subpoena power itself only arrives when the new Congress is seated in January. Planned DOGE/contracts inquiry (~$6.3B in contracts during Musk's DOGE months, per Democracy Defenders Fund) is unchanged.
  • Merger talks moved to price and control: Bloomberg (Nov 12, reported Nov 14–15): Tesla–SpaceX talks are now negotiating price and control, SpaceX taking over Tesla for stock, possible announcement as early as December; neither company has commented. Next calendar points: Tesla shareholder meeting Dec 3 and a SpaceX share expiration Dec 8. One market write-up had SPCX -2.0% to $136.26 on the week on deal uncertainty — regulatory scrutiny under the incoming Congress is the stated overhang.
  • Price still says premium fading: Cash closes Fri Nov 13: TSLA $390.22, SPCX $139.11. Hyperliquid 7d: TSLA +7.3%, SPCX +2.4% (perp marks, weekend). The post-midterm drawdown has largely retraced; the risk now sits in dated events (Nov 30 letter deadline, Dec merger window) rather than in the tape.

4. Restructuring / rotation & macro

  • Defensive rotation is the documented flow: Global consumer staples +10.6% in Oct (strongest month since 1998) and healthcare +9.8%, vs tech -13%+ since Oct began (FT data, reported Nov 10). Goldman Sachs (Nov 7): prime-book beta-adjusted net exposure at very low levels vs history, data-centre stocks -36% from highs, healthcare called "the next leg of the AI trade," and "S&P 500 ex-AI" the trade — classic post-midterm/gridlock positioning, not a crash call.
  • Fed Dec 9 is the macro hinge: Goldman's Miller flags jobs, inflation, earnings and the Fed's Dec 9 decision as the drivers. Crypto-side reporting (AInvest, Nov 14) put CME FedWatch on Nov 12 at 100% probability of a Dec 9 hike, with the dollar index at its highest since Apr 2025 — tight financial conditions are part of why leveraged crypto (ZEC above) keeps unwinding. Treat the 100% figure as reported pricing, not a settled outcome.
