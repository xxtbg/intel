1. Lame-duck Iran escalation risk

  • US struck Iran directly on Tue — Qeshm Island: US fighter jets hit an IRGC missile launch site on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Tue Nov 17 with precision munitions, per US military officials cited by CBS News, Fox News and Reuters. Officials called it self-defense after IRGC drones targeted a US radar installation in Bahrain — the third attempted Iranian strike on a US installation in five days; CENTCOM says all drones were intercepted, with no damage or injuries. An Iranian provincial official said no residential, commercial or infrastructure sites were damaged. It was the second US operation on Qeshm this month (limited strikes also on Nov 2).
  • Escalation ladder, not a campaign — so far: The strike follows Mon's UKMTO-confirmed hit on the Panama-flagged On Phoenix leaving Hormuz and Iran's unverified claim of six tankers mined/sunk since Sun. Three US carriers are now in theatre (Theodore Roosevelt arrived over the weekend). Against that, Trump told US media the US is "not going to be there too much longer" and is weighing an exit even without first reopening Hormuz; Sec. Hegseth says talks with Iran are "real" and "gaining strength" but strikes would intensify without a deal. A Qatari-mediated meeting was reported set for Nov 17, and Trump meets Xi and Putin in Shenzhen on Thu Nov 19 with the war on the agenda.
  • Dec 11 funding fight: split is now a leadership fight: Funding runs out Dec 11. Sen. Kaine is drafting language to cut off war funds in the next CR; Sens. Van Hollen, Bennet, Duckworth and Sanders (Nov 12) demand restriction language in any stopgap. But Leader Schumer has endorsed using spending leverage without demanding restrictions in this bill, Sen. Murray still backs exploring "every tool" alongside a clean short-term CR — and the dispute is feeding the Dec 1 Senate Democratic leadership election between Schumer and Van Hollen. Trump has also renewed a demand for a $350B majority-only reconciliation military bill. A CR needs 60 Senate votes; new Congress seated Jan 3, 2027.
  • Oil fell again — the strike didn't hold a premium: Cash Brent settled Mon at $92.44 and Hyperliquid marks Wed ~5:30 AM ET are lower still: BRENTOIL $89.32 (-0.7% 24h, -2.2% 7d), CL $83.19 (-1.3%, -3.2%). The IEA's Q4 surplus call (~1.7 mb/d of supply above demand, inventories rebuilding) plus recovering Gulf export flows keep capping every escalation headline; a verified cargo loss is still the missing trigger.

2. DEX / privacy inflows on regulatory & sanctions risk

  • MEXC cutoff Dec 10 — compliance clock, no new order: FinCEN's Nov 6 order (Operation Economic Outcast; $16.7B likely illicit flows May 2021–May 2026, ~$1.5B tied to Russian sanctions evasion) bars covered US institutions — banks, broker-dealers, US-registered exchanges/MSBs — from any transmittal of funds to or from MEXC Global from Dec 10, 2026, crypto included, with no cessation date. It is a special measure, not an OFAC listing or a freeze; MEXC says it is operating normally and has begun an independent AML review. US compliance teams are on the 30-day implementation clock from the Nov 10 Federal Register publication.
  • Market structure: lame duck has moved to nominations: CLARITY remains stalled after the Sep 15 Senate failure (49–50, 60 needed); crypto.news reports the remaining lame-duck action is regulator picks — Trump nominated four commissioners on Nov 12 (SEC: Steven Levine and Kristin Johnson; CFTC: Nathan Anonick and Matthew Rowland). Sen. Lummis (Nov 15) attacked Democrats for demanding a mandatory SEC registration "front door" for token issuers while voting the bill down. The House Democrats' own stricter-DeFi bill (more SEC, less CFTC, tighter DeFi and ethics rules, per Paradigm's Slaughter) is still reported outlines only — no text.
  • Privacy/DEX tape: deleveraging, not inflows: Hyperliquid Wed ~5:30 AM ET — ZEC $938.63 (+1.3% 24h, -5.1% 7d, $323.0M vol), XMR $505.99 (-0.3%, -12.7%, $33.1M), HYPE $68.66 (-0.3%, -5.1%, $430.6M). Positioning is still being cut into Dec 10, not added.

3. Musk companies — political risk premium

  • No new probe, subpoena or deal terms: Nothing new surfaced in this run's searches. The standing exposures remain DOGE-era conduct and federal-contract reviews, Cybercab/FSD scrutiny, and Starlink/Starshield dependence — all live only once the new Congress is seated Jan 3. SpaceX's IPO prospectus already warns party control can affect contracts and regulatory treatment.
  • Merger: Dec 3 is still the next marker: Both boards' special committees are still exploring a Tesla–SpaceX deal with no terms agreed. Congress cannot veto a private merger, but hearings and subpoenas can slow the committee, regulatory and shareholder steps; a deal needs a majority of all ~3.95B Tesla shares outstanding. Barclays has suggested an announcement could come before Tesla's Dec 3 annual meeting.
  • Tape: premium keeps bleeding out: Hyperliquid Wed ~5:30 AM ET — TSLA $384.28 (+2.0% 24h, -0.6% 7d), SPCX $142.50 (+4.3%, -3.3%). That extends the pattern in the Motley Fool's Nov 14 recap: the two names shed >$420B combined in the two sessions after the Democratic sweep, then recovered most of it. The political discount is now a slow repricing, not a live collapse trade — "collapse" remains a market theory, not an established fact.

4. Restructuring & macro

  • Rotation out of tech into defensives is the dominant flow: AJ Bell's super-sector analysis has information technology going from best in H1 (+28.4%) to worst in H2 (down >11% since Oct), with healthcare and consumer staples the best H2 performers after energy. HSBC calls the rotation "extreme" and the AI selloff overdone — Walmart at ~40x forward earnings vs Nvidia at ~13x after a 45% semiconductor de-rating since June. Post-midterm commentary also flags healthcare and clean energy regaining favour on the policy shift, and defense valuations exposed to fiscal scrutiny.
  • Fed hike pricing is the macro driver: After hot October CPI (+0.4% headline, +0.3% core — a third straight upside surprise), PPI +6.5% y/y and payrolls +165k vs ~65k expected, markets price ~90–95% odds of a 25bp hike at the Dec 8–9 FOMC and a small chance of 50bp; Gov. Waller has floated 50bp if core stays hot. The 10-year yield sits ~5.12–5.14%, the dollar near 103.5. FOMC minutes land today (Wed) at 2 PM ET; October PCE on Nov 25 is the next settler.
  • Equities and gold under pressure; Nvidia tonight is the test: Mon: S&P 500 -1.9%, Nasdaq -2.5% (into correction from the October peak), Dow -739 pts.
