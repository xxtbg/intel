1. Lame-duck Iran escalation risk

  • Another tanker hit in Hormuz — Nov 18 evening: A tanker outbound through the Strait of Hormuz took an unknown projectile in its engine room about 6 nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman, at 1645 UTC Nov 18, per UKMTO via reporting Nov 19; crew were reported safe in that account, origin and responsibility were not assigned, and damage/environmental impact were unknown. Iran also fired on a US airbase in Kuwait and several times at Bahrain on Wed, described as retaliation for Tue's US self-defense strike on an IRGC missile site on Qeshm Island.
  • Iran hardening after the midterms, escalation plans drafted: The New York Times, reported Nov 18, says Tehran has hardened its negotiating position since the Nov 3 midterms — reading Republican losses as a rebuke of the war — believes Hormuz disruption inflicted economic pain that paid off, is in no hurry to concede, and has drafted escalation plans including possible tanker attacks. The Strait remains the sticking point: Iran wants sanctions relief and Arab payments before restoring free shipping; Washington insists vessel attacks stop first.
  • Trump: deal talk and strike options both live: Trump said on Fox any deal must be lasting and prevent Iran rebuilding military/nuclear capability, said "we'll finish it off — soon, one way or the other," and confirmed contact with Iranian representatives. Separately, Axios reported Nov 19 Trump is still considering a joint attack with Israel, with the White House asking Pentagon/intelligence for assessments of Iranian responses — no decision or timetable. A US official said economic pressure does not make major combat operations necessary, echoing Trump's Nov 14 line that new major strikes were "extremely unlikely."
  • Dec 11 funding fight — defund amendment formalized: Sens. Duckworth, Warren and Gillibrand (Nov 13) announced an amendment to any funding deal that would defund the Iran war until Congress authorizes it, and withhold Defense Secretary travel funding until DoD reports on readiness impact. It mirrors the Nov 12 Van Hollen/Bennet/Duckworth/Sanders demand for restriction language. Funding expires Dec 11; a stopgap needs 60 Senate votes in the lame-duck chamber; new Congress seated Jan 3, 2027.
  • Oil premium still not holding: Reuters Nov 19: Brent was steady at $92.12 (+0.18% at 0237 GMT) after falling 1.3% Wed, WTI fell 3.2% Wed to its lowest since Aug 26, as Hormuz crude shipments returned to roughly pre-war levels and surplus expectations grew; EIA stocks rose 3.4M bbl to 417.8M in the week to Nov 13. Hyperliquid marks are lower: BRENTOIL $89.64 (+0.2% 24h, -0.8% 7d), CL $82.59 (-0.9%, -2.8%). Iran's navy claim of three tankers disabled by mines east of Hormuz remains unverified in Western reporting.

2. DEX / privacy inflows on regulatory & sanctions risk

  • MEXC cutoff Dec 10 — clock unchanged, no new order: FinCEN's Nov 6 order under Operation Economic Outcast named MEXC Global a primary money laundering concern (at least $16B / $16.7B in likely illicit flows May 2021–May 2026 in Treasury's findings, ~$1.5B Russian sanctions evasion, ~$1B+ in dealings with 40+ Iranian exchanges) and bars covered US institutions from any transmittal of funds to/from MEXC 30 days after Federal Register publication — effective Dec 10, 2026 per prior editions. It is not an OFAC listing or a freeze.
  • Market structure still stalled; agencies moving alone: CLARITY failed Senate cloture 49–50 in September. Since then the CFTC has proposed voluntary Regulation CTX/CAM frameworks (60-day comment) and FinCEN withdrew the 2020 unhosted-wallet $10,000 proposal and the 2023 mixing proposal. House Democrats' own SEC-led bill — stricter DeFi, more SEC / less CFTC, ethics rules — remains reported outlines only, no text. No lame-duck revival was reported today.
  • Privacy/DEX tape: still no inflow bid: Hyperliquid Thu ~5:30 AM ET — ZEC $948.44 (+1.2% 24h, -2.3% 7d, $200.9M vol), XMR $501.60 (-1.0%, -9.0%, $21.9M), HYPE $68.39 (-0.2%, -3.3%, $446.9M). Keel: Hyperliquid traded $9.7B on Nov 18 with $11.0B open interest across all markets, last 7 days averaging $11.4B/day — venue activity is holding, but privacy positioning keeps unwinding from September/October highs rather than building ahead of Dec 10.

3. Musk companies — political risk premium

  • Musk distances himself at DealBook — "pretty foolish," "not MAGA": Reuters Nov 19: Musk told the NYT DealBook Summit Wed he felt "pretty foolish" about spending nearly half a billion dollars backing Republicans in the midterms, said he would have spent less in hindsight, and — in wider summit coverage — said "I'm not MAGA." Of 37 Republican House candidates he backed, three won; six of seven Senate picks lost. In Texas, Paxton lost to Talarico and Abbott lost the governor's race to Hinojosa.
  • Scrutiny queue is explicit, but starts Jan 3: Incoming Democratic lawmakers have signaled probes into DOGE, federal contracts, and Starlink/Starshield in Ukraine; Texas officials have pledged to look at a state broadband grant benefiting SpaceX. The Motley Fool's Nov 14 post-mortem still frames the episode: TSLA/SPCX shed a combined $420B in two days after the sweep on probe fears, then recovered most of it — the premium is event-driven, not a steady discount. "Collapse" remains a theory, not an established fact.
  • Merger talk advances — Musk: "probably better together": At DealBook, Musk said Tesla and SpaceX would probably be better together on AI/robotics/chip grounds (Terafab first test chip May/June 2027), but that a merger should not itself trigger his 2025 pay awards (423.7M shares). Independent-director committees were formed in October; an announcement could come as early as December or slip to early 2027, and needs shareholder votes. Both stocks rose a second session Wed in cash trading (TSLA +0.95% to $383.72, SPCX +1.27% to $143.50 at Nov 18 close).
  • Perp tape confirms the bounce, not a premium unwind to zero: Hyperliquid Thu ~5:30 AM ET — TSLA $390.30 (+1.6% 24h, +0.3% 7d, $26.3M vol), SPCX $146.90 (+3.2%, +5.4%, $100.9M). SPCX is now above pre-election levels on the 7d window; TSLA is flat. DealBook contrition plus merger optionality is outweighing probe risk this week — until a subpoena or a formal merger filing lands.

4. Post-midterm restructuring / other market movers

  • Rotation, not exit: LGT Wealth (post-midterm note): leadership is rotating beneath falling headline indices — healthcare and consumer staples outperforming the S&P 500, equal-weight S&P near all-time highs while cap-weight lags, and hardware/data-centre names under pressure on post-midterm data-centre regulation questions. Private-client commentary (DFO, Nov) frames the same five rotations: AI from infrastructure to earnings, healthcare/clean energy regaining favour, fiscal/geopolitical (defense/oil), lower yields, and a weaker dollar supporting international diversification.
  • Rates are the brake: Strategy notes for the week to Nov 13 put the 10Y at 5.15% (from 4.86%), cash raised to overweight, long duration cut — a late-cycle, rate-driven tape. That, plus surplus oil, explains GOLD $3,731 (-1.0% 24h, -5.4% 7d) failing as a war hedge this week and SP500 (Hyperliquid xyz:SP500) $7,410.8 (+0.3%, -2.3% 7d, $367.5M vol) drifting rather than breaking.
