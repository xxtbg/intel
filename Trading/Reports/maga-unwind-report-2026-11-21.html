Post-Midterm Market Report

Saturday, November 21, 2026. Prices: Hyperliquid, ~5:30 AM ET. *TSLA/SPCX figures are Hyperliquid perp marks (US cash market closed — weekend).

1. Lame-duck Iran escalation risk

  • Trump punts the strike decision to 2027: Trump told an Israeli outlet he is not rushing decisions on Iran and may not decide until 2027, saying he wants to see what happens after Jan 1; he called Iran's terms unacceptable. Iran's foreign minister said Tehran still wants a diplomatic end but needs guarantees Washington is serious. That weakens the lame-duck escalation case on the US side — even as CBS reports the US and Israel are weighing a renewed attack in the near future, and the New York Times has reported planning for renewed strikes. (One Turn, Nov 21; TradingView/dpa-AFX, Nov 20; AND Magazine Morning Brief, Nov 20)
  • Hormuz tempo stays at roughly one attack a day: Shipping monitors reported six attacks on vessels in the past week and nearly 20 in November so far (UKMTO via TradingView); a tanker caught fire near the strait on Friday, and Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it shot down a US MQ-9 drone over Hormuz. Tehran's claim that two supertankers struck naval mines remains unconfirmed by any maritime authority, insurer or flag state. (One Turn, Nov 21; TradingView/dpa-AFX, Nov 20; AND Magazine, Nov 20)
  • War Powers fails again; blockade widens: The Senate on Thursday rejected the House-passed resolution directing the president to withdraw US forces from hostilities with Iran unless Congress authorizes them. A Pentagon budget official told the Senate Armed Services Committee the war has cost $48.3B as of Nov 13. CENTCOM warned vessels receiving Iranian cargo via ship-to-ship transfers will face military action, and the US sanctioned four Iranian oil trustees. (AND Magazine, Nov 20; One Turn, Nov 21; TradingView/dpa-AFX, Nov 20)
  • Oil still prices surplus, not war: Cash Brent settled Friday at $92.57 (+0.6% on Hormuz short-covering) but fell 3.4% on the week — a third straight weekly loss, down roughly a fifth in November — as Hormuz shipments sit near pre-war levels and the IEA sees a ~2M bpd Q4 surplus. Hyperliquid tells the same story with a weekend bounce: BRENTOIL $89.95 (+2.2% 24h, −3.7% 7d), CL $83.71 (+3.1% 24h, −4.2% 7d). Next supply test: OPEC+ ministerial, Sun Nov 29. (Reuters via Euronext/Rigzone, Nov 20; FinanceFeeds, Nov 21)

2. DEX / privacy-coin inflows on regulatory & sanctions risk

  • MEXC ban: 19 days out, no change: FinCEN's Nov 6 order (Operation Economic Outcast, FR Doc. 2026-22962, published Nov 10) bars US institutions from transmitting funds to/from MEXC Global effective Dec 10, 2026, with no end date, and explicitly covers crypto transfers. FinCEN assesses at least $16.7B in likely illicit flows May 2021–May 2026, including ~$1.5B tied to Russian sanctions evasion, plus Iran and North Korean facilitators. (FinCEN via BeVerified/ITIN, Nov 2026)
  • CLARITY still frozen; CFTC opens a side door: As of Nov 18 there has been no new Senate vote on the CLARITY Act since the failed 49–50 cloture vote on Sep 15; the bill expires with the 119th Congress on Jan 3, 2027. In parallel, the CFTC is taking comments until Dec 14 on an optional federal registration category for crypto exchanges offering leveraged/margined retail trading — a regulatory path that does not need Congress. (Banking on Bitcoin, Nov 18)
  • Tape: still no inflow bid: The complex sold off with everything else into the weekend. ZEC $746.12 (−10.7% 24h, −17.3% 7d) on a heavy $632.0M day volume; HYPE $58.59 (−9.8% 24h, −14.7% 7d) was the edition's volume leader at $898.8M; XMR $466.44 (−1.5% 24h, −12.7% 7d) was the relative outperformer, consistent with the split seen Nov 11 — ZEC trading as high-beta institutional beta, XMR as the usage-driven privacy asset. Dec 10 remains the thesis test. (Hyperliquid, Nov 21; Binance Square, Nov 11)
  • Longer-dated overhang: EU anti-money-laundering rules bar regulated providers from handling anonymity-enhancing cryptocurrencies from Jul 1, 2027 — a slow-burn delisting risk for XMR that sits behind the nearer US catalysts. (CoinPost, Nov 3)

3. Musk political risk premium (TSLA / SPCX)

  • Friday was the merger saga's worst cash session: Tesla closed Nov 20 at $345.82 (−7.64%) and SpaceX at $127.79 (−7.69%) on Nasdaq. Hyperliquid weekend perp marks have bounced partway back — TSLA $351.78 (−4.5% 24h on the rolling HL window, −9.5% 7d), SPCX $129.72 (−3.1% 24h, −4.7% 7d) — but both remain well below mid-November levels. *Hyperliquid perp marks; US cash market closed. (Motley Fool, Nov 20; Hyperliquid, Nov 21)
  • The vote math is the story now: A merger needs "yes" from more than half of ALL of Tesla's 3.95B outstanding shares — every abstention effectively counts as a no. Add a large retail base, ISS's record of opposing Musk/Tesla proposals, likely lawsuits and antitrust/political scrutiny, and the odds look lower than the 80–90% Wedbush's Dan Ives assigns. Musk holds 19.8% of Tesla. No formal agreement or exchange ratio has been announced. (Motley Fool, Nov 20)
  • HSBC: control, not synergies: HSBC upgraded Tesla to Hold from Reduce and lifted its target to $359 from $157, arguing "control as the key motivation" — SpaceX as surviving entity would entrench Musk's voting power (39% of SpaceX, ~70% of its votes, vs ~20% at Tesla). Its scenario math: $507/share for Tesla at a 33% acquisition premium vs $212 standalone; SpaceX kept at Hold, $150 target; China exposure flagged as a regulatory risk. (TradingView/Seeking Alpha, Nov 20; Stocktwits, Nov 20)
  • Dec 3 meeting mechanics: Tesla's annual meeting is Thu Dec 3, 3:00 PM CT at Gigafactory Texas; the merger is absent from the ballot (directors, say-on-pay, auditor only), and the proxy voting deadline is 11:59 PM ET on Dec 2. A December announcement remains possible per Bloomberg's Nov 12 report, unconfirmed by either company; early 2027 is the other window. (Admiral Markets, Nov 20)

4. Post-midterm restructuring & other market movers

  • Sixth straight loss; IPO couldn't save the tape: The S&P 500 fell 2.0% Thursday for a sixth consecutive loss — its longest streak in two and a half years; the Dow dropped 1,101 points to 49,097, its first close below 50,000 since June; VIX ~29. Anthropic's record IPO hit Nasdaq Thursday and stocks sold off anyway. Nvidia posted a blowout quarter and still sold off; on Hyperliquid NVDA is −20.7% over 7 days. (Motley Fool Advisor Insights, Nov 20)
  • Fed: hike is the base case, size is the question: October FOMC minutes (released Nov 18–19) showed broad support for further hikes, with Governor Waller arguing a 50bp December move should be considered. Futures pricing clusters around a 25bp hike on Dec 9 (~85–96% across Nov 19–20 reads), the remainder on 50bp. Thursday's data pushed the other way — jobless claims 203K and a soft Philly Fed index (6.3 vs ~20 expected) — and the 10-year eased to ~5.05%. (Motley Fool, Nov 20; Intellectia/AP, Nov 19; NextLevel/Reuters)
  • Rotation still running: Post-midterm flow commentary continues to show money leaving AI hardware for healthcare, banks and staples — the gridlock trade. The sector map from adviser research: hospitals/Medicaid insurers and renewables get potential upside from a Democratic Congress, defense faces spending scrutiny, crypto faces two years of legislative uncertainty, and energy stays tied to White House Iran/tariff decisions Congress can do little to change. (Traders Agency, Nov 2026; Ameriprise, Nov 2026)
  • Holiday-shortened week ahead: Q3 GDP (second estimate) and October PCE land Wed Nov 25; US markets are closed Thu Nov 26 (Thanksgiving) and close at 1 PM ET Fri Nov 27. Then the December gauntlet: Nov jobs Dec 4, FOMC Dec 8–9, Nov CPI Dec 10 — the same day the MEXC ban takes effect. (Motley Fool, Nov 20)
