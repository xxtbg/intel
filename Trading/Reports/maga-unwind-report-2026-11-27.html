1. Lame-duck Iran escalation risk

  • More claimed mine strikes in Hormuz: The IRGC said two oil tankers exploded and caught fire after striking sea mines north of Oman's Khasab port on Thursday — one a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels — and separately said an LNG carrier hit a mine and exploded. UKMTO separately reported an explosion from an unknown projectile near a tanker ~50 nautical miles west of Dubai. The mine claims are Iranian-sourced and not independently confirmed; no vessel has been named. (Reuters via The Hourly News, Nov 26; Zot News, Nov 27)
  • Tehran tightening the strait: Iran's army chief ordered the navy to close Hormuz "further day by day," and a Guards spokesman said the force is using heavy firepower to keep the strait closed to pressure the US economy. UKMTO data show 30+ maritime attacks in October and at least 22 in November — the highest levels since the war began. Tehran says it could reopen the strait within 48 hours if Washington meets its conditions; Trump has signaled he is inclined to restart strikes in the new year absent a deal. (Miscw / CNN data, Nov 27)
  • Saudi infrastructure hit again: A projectile struck Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery on Thursday, killing a Nepalese worker, wounding several others and temporarily halting some operations, per the Saudi Energy Ministry — a day after the Houthis claimed attacks on Ras Tanura and the Red Sea ports of Jeddah and Jazan. (Reuters via Euronext, Nov 27)
  • Oil at a three-week high: Brent settled at $96.57 Thursday — its highest close since Nov 6 — and held ~$96.19–96.61 Friday; WTI was $85.41–85.78. ANZ: Brent "can still push above USD100/bbl without another major escalation," but that needs evidence of constrained deliverable supply. OPEC+ meets Sunday and is expected to hold output targets through at least Q1 2027. Hyperliquid perp marks still lag cash futures: BRENTOIL $92.42 vs ~$96.6 cash Brent. (Reuters via 97.9 WEVE, Nov 27)

Treasury says it is also targeting the cryptocurrency exchanges Tehran is reaching for as conventional channels close — the exact flow this theory tracks. 3. Musk premium: TSLA / SPCX

  • TSLA slipped on the EU delay and merger talk: Tesla closed at $380.14 on Wednesday, Nov 25 (-1.18%, intraday low $357.64), after the EU's Technical Committee for Motor Vehicles postponed its assisted-driving meeting from Dec 1 to Dec 15 and fresh Tesla–SpaceX merger reports circulated. The Hyperliquid perp has since recovered to $388.34 (+1.6% 24h, +5.7% 7d). SPCX gained 1.30% Wednesday; its perp is $132.25 (+2.7% 24h, flat on the week). (Gotrade, Nov 26)
  • Merger scrutiny is the premium now: Both companies have formed special committees; reports describe five directors and three investment banks (Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs for SpaceX, Evercore for Tesla) weighing an all-stock combination valued at ~$3.5 trillion — with no definitive agreement, terms or exchange ratio announced. Analysis flags dilution math for SPCX holders, Musk's uneven voting stakes (~20% of Tesla, 80%+ of SpaceX), and likely lawmaker/regulator attention. Tesla booked $626M of Megapack revenue from SpaceX in the first nine months of 2026, so the two are already commercially entangled. (Motley Fool, Nov 26)
  • Political-risk repricing, ongoing: Post-midterm analysis continues to frame both names as carrying oversight risk — federal contracts and Starlink national-security work for SpaceX; autonomy approvals, incentives and robotaxi regulation for Tesla — under a Democratic Congress seated Jan 3. "Collapse" claims remain a market theory, not an established fact; the measurable effect so far is a persistent discount and headline-driven volatility. (LMBDA Research, Nov 8)

  • Rotation out of AI keeps broadening: Money has been moving from AI names into Energy (+40.9% YTD), Health Care (+20.9%) and Consumer Staples (+19.4%), with small caps and European equities also gaining leadership; biotech and banks have outperformed since the October highs. The Nov 5 AI-led selloff (S&P -4.4%, Nasdaq into correction) is still the template trade. (NBOfI Market Commentary, November 2026; Nasdaq Biweekly Investment Insights, Nov 13)
  • Fed stays restrictive into the oil shock: The funds rate sits at 3.75%–4.00% after October's hold, with markets pricing roughly a 75% chance of a December hike following the strong October jobs report (165k vs ~65k expected). An energy-driven inflation impulse landing mid-hiking-cycle is now the central macro tension: every leg higher in Brent feeds the case for the Dec 9 FOMC to tighten. (NBOfI, November 2026; Finimize / Reuters, Nov 27)
  • Safe havens bid: GOLD +3.0% 24h to $3,672.50 on Hyperliquid alongside the oil spike, and the SP500 perp is +1.3% 24h / +2.5% 7d — risk assets are not panicking; this is a commodity-and-haven move, not a broad unwind.
