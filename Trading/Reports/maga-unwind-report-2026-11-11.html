Post-Midterm Market Report

Wednesday, November 11, 2026. Prices: Hyperliquid, ~5:30 AM ET.

1. Lame-duck Iran escalation risk

  • Pause looks tactical, not terminal: Trump is holding off on fresh strikes after Gulf appeals, but US forces are still building up in the region, Qatar/Pakistan mediation continues, and Washington and Tehran remain far apart — especially on Iran's nuclear programme. Hormuz traffic has averaged 10.5M bpd since early October; the IMO tracked 131 confirmed incidents near the strait from Feb 28 to Nov 9, with 35 in October alone, the highest monthly total of the war. (Reuters column via Devdiscourse, Nov 11)
  • Oil rebounds off two-month lows: Brent futures rose 1.1% to $92.53 and WTI 0.8% to $86.71 early Wednesday after fresh shipping attacks revived supply fears — a tanker was struck northeast of Lima, Oman (UKMTO), and Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two tankers caught fire after hitting mines on an "unauthorized route." Iran's top security official Mohsen Rezaei said no negotiations are currently taking place, clawing back Tuesday's de-escalation drop (Brent had settled $91.49, lowest since Aug 31). LSEG notes even a deal would not quickly restore flows — mine clearance, insurance and shipowner willingness come first. (Reuters, Nov 11)
  • Hyperliquid oil follows: BRENTOIL $91.75 (24h +2.8%, 7d -10.2%, volume $158M), CL $86.29 (24h +2.1%, 7d -9.4%, volume $135M). The premium is returning only partly — both are still well below the Oct 30 high (~$118 Brent) — so the market is pricing a pause with live shipping risk, not a settlement.
  • Funding fight is the lame-duck lever: federal funding runs out December 11; Rep. Ro Khanna has floated a continuing resolution prohibiting financing of US combat activities in Iran, and the WSJ editorial page is urging a lame-duck reconciliation bill to fund defense through Democratic opposition. The 119th Congress sits until the 120th seats on January 3, 2027. (WSJ via Energy Musings, Nov 9; Action Network, Nov 10)

2. DEX and privacy-coin inflows — regulatory and sanctions risk

  • MEXC ban now has a date — Dec 10: FinCEN's Nov 6 order under Operation Economic Outcast (Section 9714) was published in the Federal Register on November 10, starting the 30-day clock: effective December 10, 2026, with no end date, US institutions may not transmit funds to/from MEXC Global (at least $16B–$16.7B in alleged illicit flows, May 2021–May 2026, incl. Russian sanctions evasion and Iranian/North Korean links). Binance said Nov 10 it will stop processing MEXC transactions — declining withdrawals to MEXC and holding incoming MEXC deposits for review; OKX had already warned users. Tracked MEXC reserves fell from ~$4.48B on Nov 6 to ~$4.17B on Nov 10. (Federal Register 91 FR 72420 via PlainBTC, Nov 10; Cointelegraph, updated Nov 10)
  • Market structure — unchanged: CLARITY remains stalled after the Senate's 49–50 cloture failure on Sep 15 and is assessed as dead in the lame duck; House Democrats' own bill (stricter DeFi rules, more SEC / less CFTC, Waters consumer protections, ethics curbs on officials) is still drafting with no text. It dies Jan 3 if not passed. (Unchained, Nov 5; FinanceFeeds, Nov 4; Rebatly update, Nov 10)
  • Privacy coins split hard: ZEC crashed — Hyperliquid $992.61 (24h -13.3%, 7d -23.6%, volume $714M, the heaviest in this edition) and spot trackers show ZEC -12% to -14% on the day, the worst of the privacy group (weighted -6.9%). XMR went the other way: $573.89 (24h +5.2%, 7d -10.5%, volume $37M), spot +1.2%, the group's best performer. That is rotation within privacy, not a broad sanctions-risk inflow — the thesis is still not confirming.
  • DEX tape weak: HYPE $72.26 (24h -2.5%, 7d -16.3%, volume $449M) and ARB $0.1586 (24h -4.6%, 7d -2.9%) both fell. No DEX inflow confirmation ahead of the Dec 10 MEXC deadline — if flows are going to rotate onshore/offshore around the ban, they have not shown up in price yet.

Waller set hours earlier for considering a 50bp hike. Traders now fully price a 25bp hike at the Dec 8–9 FOMC (to 4.00–4.25%), with ~13.6% odds on 50bp; the 2-year Treasury yield jumped 17.6bp to 4.74%. October is the last CPI the Fed sees before that meeting (November CPI lands Dec 10, after it). Bitcoin slipped to ~$73.8k (-1.1%) on the print. (BLS via Reuters / USA Today, Nov 10; CNBC FedWatch via Tech & Money Press, Nov 11; Analytics Insight, Nov 11)
