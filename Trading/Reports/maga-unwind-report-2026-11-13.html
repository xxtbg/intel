Post-Midterm Market Report

Friday, November 13, 2026. Prices: Hyperliquid, ~5:30 AM ET.

1. Lame-duck Iran escalation risk

  • Oil rebounds as deal hopes fade: Brent settled 3.4% higher at $95.95 overnight and WTI held near $88.80 after Trump said a deal with Iran is not necessary and that Washington will stick with the naval blockade (reports, Nov 13). Early Friday Brent eased to ~$95.54 (-0.3%) and WTI to ~$88.18 (-0.6%), still on track for a second straight weekly fall (-2.9% this week after -16% last week). The IEA says global supply overtook demand for the first time since the war began — supply rose 2.3 mb/d to 105.4 mb/d in October as Gulf exports recovered — and EIA reported US crude stocks up 4.7M barrels to 414.4M in the week ended Nov 6. OPEC output rose 1.96 mb/d to 27.16 mb/d in October, led by Saudi Arabia (10.56 mb/d).
  • Rhetoric hardens, allies restrain: Trump said Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, the UAE and Bahrain pressed him to hold off further large-scale strikes, while boasting the US could destroy Iran's energy infrastructure, bridges and manufacturing in three days, and warning of severe consequences if Iran targets US officials (following reported threats against Treasury Secretary Bessent). He also sidelined Vice President Vance from Iran diplomacy, telling him to focus on domestic priorities — a setback for back-channel talks. No new large-scale strike decision has been announced; the blockade and "status quo" remain, which keeps the lame-duck escalation tail live rather than resolved.
  • Shipping war continues underneath: Iran said its Sirik port near Hormuz was hit by enemy fire on Thursday; its military said four tankers were hit by explosions in the strait after ignoring warnings, UKMTO reported at least two tankers struck by projectiles in recent days, and Yemen's Houthis claimed attacks on Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline pumping station, a Yanbu oil facility and an Aramco building in Riyadh. Verisk Maplecroft counted 36 confirmed attacks on commercial vessels across the Gulf, Hormuz and Gulf of Oman in October. Iran's security chief also ruled out removing enriched nuclear material.
  • Hyperliquid oil follows cash up: BRENTOIL $93.14 (24h +3.3%, 7d -5.1%, volume $137M), CL $87.06 (24h +2.6%, 7d -4.0%, volume $180M). Oil is stabilising well below the Oct 30 peak, but the combination of a hardening US line, ongoing tanker hits and the Dec 11 federal funding expiry — with the new Congress seating Jan 3, 2027 — keeps a surprise strike decision as the main upside risk to crude.

2. DEX and privacy-coin inflows — regulatory and sanctions risk

  • MEXC countdown: 27 days: no change to the order — FinCEN's Nov 6 Section 9714 order under Operation Economic Outcast (at least $16B / $16.7B in alleged illicit flows, May 2021–May 2026, tied to Russia, Iran, North Korea and cybercrime) was published in the Federal Register Nov 10 and takes effect December 10, 2026, with no end date. It bars covered US financial institutions from transmitting funds to or from MEXC Global, crypto included; it does not bar individuals sending coins to their own wallets, though US exchanges may later refuse traceable coins. Binance has already stopped transfers to MEXC ahead of the effective date (update Nov 12).
  • Market structure — CLARITY dead, replacement stricter: Paradigm's Justin Slaughter (Unchained, Nov 5) still calls CLARITY dead in the lame duck; House Financial Services staff are drafting a Democratic bill with stricter DeFi rules than the BRCA, more jurisdiction shifted from the CFTC to the SEC, Waters-style consumer protections and ethics curbs on officials' crypto involvement. Any bill not passed by Jan 3 dies and must be reintroduced.
  • Privacy breaks again — still no inflow signal: ZEC $946.25 (24h -4.8%, 7d -26.7%, volume $525M — the heaviest crypto volume in this edition) fell again; spot trackers agree (ZEC ~$947, -4.8%). XMR $538.19 (24h -2.8%, 7d -13.0%, volume $92M) also fell, and was the standout decliner among majors at the Nov 12 UTC close (-5.2% to $551.89 on that cut). Capital continues to leave the privacy group ahead of Dec 10, not rotate into it.
  • DEX tape: volume holds, price mixed: Hyperliquid perps volume was ~$9.8B in 24h (trackers, Nov 13) with ~$8.0B open interest. HYPE itself: $72.46 (24h +1.1%, 7d -7.8%, volume $560M) — a small green day that narrows its 7-day loss, but no confirmation of a CEX-to-DEX rotation bid. Thesis 2 remains unconfirmed on price.

3. Musk companies — political risk premium

  • Merger talks advance — both names bid: Bloomberg reporting on Nov 12, carried by Reuters and others, says SpaceX and Tesla are working through merger terms and governance, with an announcement possible as soon as December or slipping into early 2027; separating Tesla's China manufacturing from SpaceX's defence work is a reported obstacle, and SpaceX's filing says its special committee has made no decision. Cash closes Nov 12: TSLA $397.45 (+1.69%, its best five-day run in over a year, ~+16% in five sessions) and SPCX $139.94 (+0.14%, after an intraday high of $147.60). Hyperliquid perps track it: TSLA $399.10 (24h +2.4%, 7d +13.6%, volume $72M), SPCX $140.67 (24h +0.3%, 7d +8.6%, volume $155M). *
  • Fundamentals in the filings: SpaceX reported Q3 revenue of $14.6B, up 161% year on year, and Tesla disclosed $221M of Megapack sales to SpaceX in the quarter ended Sep 30. Those figures give the merger story a business case to trade on, independent of politics — for now the merger bid is outweighing the political risk premium, and the post-midterm "collapse" claim remains a market theory, not an established outcome.
  • Oversight overhang unchanged: Democrats, who take both chambers Jan 3, have signalled subpoenas and inquiries into Musk — DOGE tenure, SpaceX federal contracts (at least $6.3B during his DOGE tenure, plus a reported $10B after), and Starlink/Starshield restrictions in Ukraine (Sen. Blumenthal's Oct 22 inquiry). No formal investigation has started; committees gain subpoena power at seating. On a merger, Congress can slow a deal with scrutiny but does not hold a veto — approval rests with boards, shareholders and regulators, with Tesla's annual meeting on December 3 the next dated catalyst.

4. Rotation and macro

  • Fed: 25bp base case, 50bp now discussed: October core CPI rose 0.3% m/m for a third straight month (headline +0.4%), exactly meeting Gov. Waller's pre-release threshold for considering a 50bp hike at the Dec 8–9 FOMC; Dallas Fed President Logan favours another 50bp in total. CME FedWatch on Nov 10 put a December hike at 91% for 25bp and 9% for 50bp. Goldman now also forecasts a March hike, and Deutsche Bank expects January as well. Next data: October PCE and the Q3 GDP second estimate on Nov 25; November CPI lands Dec 10 — after the FOMC, and the same day as the MEXC ban.
  • Defensive rotation intact, but stretched: tech remains the worst H2 sector (MSCI World Tech -7% in October, -4.6% more in November) while staples, healthcare and utilities climb; Goldman's read remains "the S&P 500 excluding AI is the trade," with healthcare as the next leg. HSBC calls the AI selloff overdone — US semis at 13.5x forward earnings after a 45% de-rating since June, versus Walmart at ~40x near a record — so the rotation is now crowded on the defensive side. Nvidia earnings next week are the next test. Hyperliquid SP500 $7,602.00 (24h +0.1%, 7d +3.0%, volume $397M) holds its recovery; GOLD $3,899.20 (24h -1.2%, 7d -4.0%, volume $94M) fell again despite the oil rebound and tanker attacks — no haven bid, with surplus supply and hike risk weighing instead.
