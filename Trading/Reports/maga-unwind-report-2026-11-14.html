1. Lame-duck Iran escalation risk

  • No new escalation yet — blockade, not strikes: Despite pre-midterm threats of imminent escalation, none has occurred so far; Trump has posted he doesn't 'need' a deal with Iran, while the US keeps its naval blockade of Iranian shipments in place. Hegseth survived a House impeachment vote tied to voter anger over the war, with only Rep. Massie among Republicans voting for it.
  • Oil down for a second week: Brent settled Fri at $95.84 (+$0.02), WTI at $88.56 (-0.2%), both ~2.5% lower on the week after -16% / -13% the prior week. Reuters ties the fall to a growing supply surplus and the blockade lowering fears of renewed direct fighting. IEA (Nov 13): world supply hit 105.4 mb/d in Oct (+2.3 mb/d m/m), Gulf exports recovered to ~82% of pre-war crude levels, first surplus since the war began Feb 28. EIA: US stocks +4.7M bbl to 414.4M (week ended Nov 6).
  • Hormuz risk still live: Iran's military said Thu four tankers were hit by explosions in Hormuz after ignoring warnings; Houthis claimed attacks on Saudi sites including the East-West pipeline pumping station, Yanbu and an Aramco building in Riyadh — none independently confirmed at settlement. A Qatari-brokered proposal to reopen Hormuz was taken to the White House ~Nov 9; Trump has since said a deal is not needed and the blockade stays. Lame-duck window runs to Jan 3, 2027 seating; Dec 18 continuing-resolution funding cliff and the War Powers / $47B cost fight are the congressional levers to watch.

2. DEX / privacy inflows on regulatory & sanctions risk

  • MEXC clock now dated: FinCEN's Nov 6 order under Operation Economic Outcast names MEXC Global a primary money-laundering concern (≥$16B illicit flows May 2021–May 2026, incl. ~$1.5B Russian sanctions evasion and ~$1B+ with 40+ Iranian exchanges). Updated guidance puts the US financial-institution cutoff at Dec 10, 2026 (30 days after Federal Register publication). OKX has warned users not to send funds directly from MEXC — transfers may be frozen / accounts investigated — an early sign of de-risking spreading beyond US banks.
  • Market structure flips SEC-ward: House Democrats are drafting their own market-structure bill to replace the failed CLARITY Act (failed 49–50 in Sept), giving the SEC — not the CFTC — the lead, building on Waters' investor-protection amendment plus ethics rules aimed at officials. Stricter DeFi treatment is the direction of travel under the incoming majority, which supports the DEX/privacy bid in theory even as prices fall near-term.
  • Privacy prices: sharp 7-day unwind, not inflow: On Hyperliquid, ZEC -30.0% in 7d to $901.99 ($368M day vol), XMR -14.4% to $525.21, HYPE -11.2% to $69.21 ($693M day vol). ZEC broke $1,000 Nov 11 for the first time since Sept amid ~$35.7M long liquidations and Grayscale ZCSH outflows ($46.25M in Oct, >$15M early Nov). XMR rose the day ZEC fell — the sector is trading as two different trades, not one. Hyperliquid L1 DEX volume: $372.7M in 24h, $1.61B in 7d (DefiLlama, Nov 14) — no breakout inflow print yet.
